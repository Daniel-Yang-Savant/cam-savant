Cardiac rehabilitation after coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) or myocardial infarction (MI) combines monitored exercise, medication and risk-factor management, education, and support for return to daily life.

## Sternum protection after open surgery

If the sternum was divided, follow the surgical team’s precautions, commonly for about 6–8 weeks. Avoid movements that cause pain, clicking, or instability. Older protocols may use fixed arm and lifting limits; many programs now emphasize keeping loads close, moving both arms together, and using pain and sternum stability as guides. Your surgeon’s instruction takes priority.

Do not push forcefully through both arms to rise from a chair, perform heavy overhead work, or lift beyond the prescribed limit.

## Phase 1: Hospital recovery (days 1–7)

Use the incentive spirometer if prescribed, practice diaphragmatic breathing, and support the incision with a pillow for coughing. Sit up, stand, and walk under supervision as soon as medically stable.

Monitor heart rhythm, blood pressure, symptoms, and sternum stability. Postoperative atrial fibrillation is common and must be reported.

Gentle neck, trunk, and arm movement may be performed within a pain-free, protected range. Practice standing using leg strength rather than forceful arm pushing.

## Phase 2: Early outpatient recovery (weeks 2–6)

Formal phase II cardiac rehabilitation may use walking or stationary cycling for 20–30 minutes at a submaximal intensity, with ECG monitoring when indicated.

Use the talk test or an individualized rating of perceived exertion. Warm up and cool down. Build duration before intensity.

Continue gentle upper-body and thoracic mobility without heavy pulling. Monitor the chest incision and any leg graft-harvest site for redness, swelling, heat, pain, or drainage.

## Phase 3: Intermediate training (weeks 6–12)

After sternum healing is confirmed, gradually add upper-body loading. Aerobic exercise may progress toward 45–60 minutes and a higher individualized intensity based on symptoms, medications, and exercise testing.

Begin low-load resistance training for major muscle groups, avoiding breath holding and any movement that causes sternum pain or clicking. An exercise stress test may help set safe intensity.

## Phase 4: Long-term maintenance (after month 3)

Transition to a sustainable home or community program with aerobic activity and moderate-load, higher-repetition resistance training. Return to swimming, golf, cycling, and sexual activity depends on sternum stability, symptoms, and medical clearance.

Long-term care includes medication adherence, smoking cessation, blood-pressure, lipid and diabetes management, a heart-healthy eating pattern, sleep, stress management, and regular follow-up. Targets must be individualized by the cardiology team.

## Stop exercise and seek help for

- Chest pressure or pain spreading to the arm, shoulder, back, or jaw
- Severe shortness of breath or symptoms at rest
- Palpitations with dizziness, fainting, or near-fainting
- New sternum pain, clicking, instability, or wound drainage
- Rapid weight gain, worsening leg swelling, or increasing breathlessness

Call emergency services for symptoms suggesting a heart attack or other acute emergency.

## Summary

Cardiac rehabilitation is more than walking. It combines safe progressive exercise, sternum and wound care, risk-factor management, and long-term adherence to treatment.
