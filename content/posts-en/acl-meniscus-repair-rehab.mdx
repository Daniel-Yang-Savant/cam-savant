Combining ACL reconstruction with meniscus repair changes the rehabilitation plan. The ACL graft needs progressive loading, but the repaired meniscus must also be protected from compression, shear, and twisting. The exact restrictions depend on whether the anterior horn, posterior horn, body, or meniscal root was repaired and on the surgeon’s technique.

## Phase 1: Protection (weeks 1–4)

Many protocols require non-weight bearing or toe-touch weight bearing with crutches. The brace is commonly locked in full extension for walking.

Knee flexion is usually limited to 0–90 degrees during the first four weeks. Do not force the knee beyond the surgeon’s limit.

Early exercises focus on maintaining circulation and muscle activation:

- Quadriceps sets
- Ankle pumps
- Straight-leg raises with the brace locked, provided there is no quadriceps lag
- Gentle hip and trunk exercises that do not load or rotate the operated knee

Avoid squatting, pivoting, kneeling, deep flexion, and unsupported walking.

## Phase 2: Transition to weight bearing (weeks 4–8)

Weight bearing often progresses from partial loading around week 4 toward full weight bearing as pain, swelling, and control permit. Crutches may be discontinued around weeks 6–8 only when your surgeon approves and your gait is normal.

After week 4, flexion may gradually progress beyond 90 degrees, with a goal of full motion near week 8 if healing and symptoms allow.

Once full, pain-free weight bearing has been established, protected strengthening may begin:

- Mini-squats from 0–45 degrees
- Closed-chain exercises within the approved knee-flexion range
- Calf, hip, and core strengthening

Do not use pain relief as permission to advance faster than the repair biology allows.

## Phase 3: Intermediate strengthening (months 2–3)

The brace may be discontinued for daily activity when quadriceps control is adequate and gait is normal. Progress closed-chain strengthening, usually keeping loaded knee flexion within 0–60 degrees initially. Add balance, proprioception, and core-control exercises.

A stationary bicycle may be introduced when sufficient motion is available for a smooth revolution. Increase resistance gradually and monitor for joint-line pain or swelling.

## Phase 4: Advanced rehabilitation (months 4–6)

Progress dynamic strengthening, lunges, movement-control drills, and carefully selected agility tasks. Straight-line jogging is generally delayed until approximately months 5–6, later than after an isolated ACL reconstruction.

Before higher loads, the knee should have no joint-line tenderness or effusion and should demonstrate good alignment and control. Cutting and pivoting are introduced only after the repair has had adequate healing time.

Return to competitive sport is commonly delayed until 9–12 months and requires objective strength, hop, movement-quality, and confidence testing.

## How this differs from isolated ACL reconstruction

**Weight bearing**
An isolated ACL reconstruction may allow weight bearing as tolerated. A combined meniscus repair often requires non-weight bearing or toe-touch loading during the first four weeks.

**Crutch use**
Crutches may be removed around week 4 after isolated ACL surgery, but commonly remain until weeks 6–8 after combined repair.

**Flexion**
Flexion is usually held at 90 degrees or less for the first four weeks to protect the repaired meniscus.

**Running and sport**
Jogging and return to pivoting sport occur later because both the graft and the meniscus must heal.

## Key precautions

- The first six weeks are especially important for meniscal healing. Follow weight-bearing restrictions exactly.
- Stop and seek review for increasing joint-line pain, swelling, locking, catching, or loss of motion.
- Avoid twisting even when ordinary walking is painless.
- Meniscal root repair often requires an even more conservative protocol.

## Summary

This combined procedure needs patience. Protect the repair early, restore motion and gait gradually, and use objective testing rather than the calendar alone before returning to running or sport.
